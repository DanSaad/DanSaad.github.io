I recently consulted with a local dental practice to discuss their HIPAA compliance relating to recent changes to to the HIPAA Security Rule. I aimed to provide professional help in understanding the "what" and "how" of the new and upcoming regulatory changes and to conduct an initial assessment to set up a full Security Risk Assessment (SRA).

### Background and Approach
To prepare for this initial consultation, I drew on two primary areas of my background. My time as a COVID-19 Contact Tracer in Jersey City (certified by Johns Hopkins University) gave me a fundamental understanding of HIPAA’s role in protecting sensitive health information. Complementing that, my Google Cybersecurity Professional Certificate provided the technical framework for understanding risk, data integrity, and network security.

### Research and Discovery
Because HIPAA regulations are constantly evolving, I needed to ensure my advice was based on the most current information. I spent time reviewing the 2026 regulatory updates, categorizing new requirements as either passed (now under enforcement) or proposed (scheduled for enforcement in 2027). 

My research process involved:
*   **Watching HHS and OCR webinars:** I looked for the "plain English" explanations of the new Privacy and Security Rule updates.
*   **Reviewing Official Documentation:** I cross-referenced these webinars with official government press releases to ensure the requirements were accurate.
*   **Analyzing the SRA Framework:** I spent time with the official HHS Security Risk Assessment (SRA) spreadsheet. It is a dense document, and my goal was to determine how to make it manageable for a small business owner.
*   ***Reading NIST Materials:** I read the NIST 800-66 standard, which details how to implement the Security Rule.
*   ***Consulting Subject Matter Experts:** I read multiple articles from The HIPAA Journal and professionals in the sub-field of HIPAA Cybersecurity for small practices (including legal and cybersecurity professionals).

### Translating Complexity into Action
One of the key goals of this initial consultation was to determine how best to help the client conduct the SRA. I began by identifying which parts of the SRA were administrative (which I could handle with the practice owner) and which were technical (which would need the cooperation of the practice's IT contractor). 

I formulated a simplified checklist for the client to provide to their IT service provider. By breaking down the complex requirements into specific "technical asks"—such as requirements for encryption standards or specific log retention—I could show the client exactly how I would bridge the gap between high-level government regulations and their daily IT operations.

### Conclusion
This was a discovery phase designed to establish a roadmap. By doing this preliminary deep dive, I was able to give the client a clear picture of the work ahead, the timeline for the 2027 implementation, and a clear indication that I could successfully lead them through a full, comprehensive Security Risk Assessment.
