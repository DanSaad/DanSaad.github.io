Before moving into cybersecurity, I worked as a COVID-19 Case Investigator and Contact Tracer for the Jersey City Department of Health from April 2021 to April 2022. It wasn't a security role, but looking back, it involved many of the same skills used in incident response.

My main responsibilities were investigating positive cases, identifying close contacts, and helping prevent further spread. I worked with confidential health data and had to follow HIPAA and state communicable disease rules closely.

When a lab result came in (through the state's CDRSS system), I first verified the information in the surveillance system, confirmed the person's jurisdiction, and determined whether they were symptomatic or asymptomatic. Based on that, I calculated their infectious period (48 hours prior to symptom onset for symptomatic cases, or 48 hours prior to a positive test for asymptomatic unless hints could be found of a first exposure).

I then interviewed each person, asking about their activities and locations during the infectious period to identify a potential site of infection and close contacts -- people they had been within six feet of for at least 10 minutes cumulatively over a 24-hour period. Contacts could be from work, school, household, social gatherings, healthcare visits, or public transit.

After identifying contacts, I notified them of their exposure, explained what quarantine and isolation meant, and connected them to resources like food delivery, medication delivery, telehealth support, or wage replacement programs if they needed to isolate.

For higher-risk situations -- like outbreaks in long-term care facilities, schools, or workplaces -- I escalated cases to my supervisor, so that the city could organized a wider-scale response. I documented every step in the CDRSS system within the required timeframes.

This experience taught me how to triage quickly (prioritizing cases based on risk factors), investigate systematically (building timelines and identifying exposure vectors), communicate clearly under pressure, and maintain strict confidentiality when handling sensitive data. It also showed me the importance of documentation, follow-through, and learning from each incident to improve the process.

While the tools and threats are different, the core incident response lifecycle -- preparation, detection, analysis, containment, eradication, recovery, and lessons learned -- maps closely to what I did as a contact tracer. The discipline of treating every case with urgency and care has carried over into how I approach security alerts and investigations today.
